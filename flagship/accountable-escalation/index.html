Escalation is not failure. It is the boundary doing its work.

An exception is a condition in which ordinary authority is no longer sufficient. The cost of an escalation path is small compared with the cost of silently converting uncertain evidence, an expanded scope, or another person’s changed rights into routine execution.

01 / SIGNAL

Name the stop condition

Define the evidence, threshold, conflict, uncertainty, policy exception, or rights change that makes routine progression inappropriate.

02 / HOLD

Prevent silent continuation

When the condition appears, stop the action at a known safe point and preserve the context needed for the person who must decide.

03 / ROUTE

Assign the decision owner

Route to a person or governed decision group with the actual right to resolve the exception—not merely the nearest operator.

04 / RESTART

Record the return condition

State what was decided, what changed, what new limit applies, and whether the workflow may resume, remain held, or be retired.

ACCOUNTABILITY BOUNDARY

A human checkpoint is meaningful only when the person has enough context, authority, and time to make a real decision—not when approval is a decorative click-through.
