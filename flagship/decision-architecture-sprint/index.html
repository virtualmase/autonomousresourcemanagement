DECISION ARCHITECTURE / FOUR WEEKS / ONE PATH

Bring the resource decision that should not travel unexamined.

SPRINT BOUNDARYOne consequential AI or cloud resource decision. No production change, access grant, spend approval, vendor selection, or authority expansion occurs through this engagement.
01THE READER
QUESTION

When a workflow begins to shape cost, capacity, or another person’s options, the hard problem is not the configuration.

It is whether the underlying decision can be inspected. Which signal is meaningful enough to influence a resource action? What is the cost or capacity boundary? Who owns the exception? When must a system hold rather than proceed? The ARM Decision Architecture Sprint makes those conditions explicit for one live decision path.

This is a working engagement for an executive sponsor, decision owner, platform or AI operator, and FinOps or financial counterpart. It produces a versionable control design and an accountable 90-day handoff; it does not substitute for the client’s authorization, compliance review, or operational responsibility.

02WORKING
METHOD

The sprint follows the decision, not the org chart.

Each week leaves a concrete record. Scope remains fixed to one workload or resource decision, so uncertainty becomes visible rather than spreading into a generic transformation programme.

  1. WEEK / 01

    Locate the decision

    Map the resource, operating trigger, consequence, affected owner, existing control, and evidence gap.

    DECISION BOUNDARY MAP
  2. WEEK / 02

    Write the signal contract

    Define semantic meaning, source, freshness, threshold, resource effect, safe state, exception route, and accountable owner.

    RESOURCE SIGNAL CONTRACT
  3. WEEK / 03

    Set the envelope

    Relate consumption to unit economics; declare expected, guardrail, and stop conditions; review autonomy and escalation.

    COST ENVELOPE + PROGRESSION REVIEW
  4. WEEK / 04

    Hand off the control

    Reconcile trade-offs, identify the smallest responsible next action, and assign the first 90-day control milestone.

    EXECUTIVE MAP + CONTROL BACKLOG
03WHAT WILL
REMAIN

The outcome is not a recommendation. It is a record of how the next decision should be made.

The Sprint creates working artifacts that distinguish observation, calculation, assumption, boundary, authority, exception, and review. They are designed for a client-controlled, versioned location and remain subject to the client’s own change, risk, and approval processes.

01 / MAP

Decision boundary

Names the allowed decision, affected resource, excluded actions, success and guardrail measures, safe state, owner, and missing evidence.

02 / SIGNAL

Resource signal contract

Explains what each decision-relevant signal means, when it becomes stale or unreliable, and what it may—and may not—influence.

03 / ENVELOPE

Cost and capacity limits

Relates consumption to the relevant unit of value, then records expected range, guardrail, stop/escalate condition, and pre-approved degraded behavior.

04 / AUTHORITY

Autonomy progression review

Separates observation, recommendation, constrained action, and closed-loop authority; each advancement requires evidence, pause, exception, and reauthorization conditions.

05 / HANDOFF

Ninety-day control backlog

Assigns the next minimum implementation action, dependency, owner, review point, and unresolved condition without implying that an external advisor has authorized it.

04ENTRY
CONDITION

A useful sprint starts with a decision that already has consequences.

USE THIS WHEN
  • A model-routing policy, autoscaling mechanism, AI gateway, agentic workflow, or capacity decision must move forward within 90 days.
  • A senior sponsor can convene an operating owner and relevant platform/AI and FinOps counterparts.
  • The organization can identify an actual resource, a likely decision consequence, and an acceptable safe state.
DO NOT USE THIS WHEN
  • The ask is a generic AI strategy, an unbounded cloud transformation, or a free architecture review.
  • The real objective is to bypass an existing authorization, procurement, security, financial, or change-management process.
  • The decision cannot be named, no owner can be reached, or no evidence can be made available even in a sanitized form.
05SCOPE
DISCUSSION

The first question is whether the work should proceed at all.

A scope discussion tests five conditions before any engagement is considered. It is deliberately short and does not collect production information through this public page.

  1. 01

    What resource decision is already being made or proposed?

  2. 02

    Which signal is trusted now, and which signal is missing or disputed?

  3. 03

    What could go wrong if that decision were wrong for seven days?

  4. 04

    Who owns the operational, economic, and exception boundary?

  5. 05

    What must be decided in the next 90 days?
