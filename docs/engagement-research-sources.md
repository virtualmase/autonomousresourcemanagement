# ARM Engagement, Trust, and Attention: Evidence Notes

**Status:** Research input for the ARM engagement and visual-asset system.  
**Use:** Design for a qualified reader’s legitimate next task, not compulsive attention capture, untruthful scarcity, concealed commercial influence, or avoidable data collection.

## Evidence and bounded implications

| Topic | Evidence | What the ARM system may responsibly infer | What it must not claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Web credibility** | In a study of 2,684 people evaluating two live websites, site “design look” appeared in 46.1% of comments made about credibility; information structure and focus also appeared prominently. [1] | Visual finish and information organization can affect whether a reader gives a source enough attention to evaluate its substantive evidence. | A polished design proves no claim, expertise, safety, or effectiveness. |
| **First visual impression** | Lindgaard et al. reported that visual-appeal ratings were closely correlated between 50 ms and 500 ms exposures. [2] | The opening composition must establish legible hierarchy, subject relevance, and material quality immediately. | There is no universal “50 ms conversion” rule or evidence that visual novelty alone makes buyers trust a site. |
| **Information scent** | Information-foraging research describes users estimating likely information value against the time/effort of obtaining it. Titles, visible copy, images, and link labels contribute to the perceived “scent” of a source. [3] | Page labels and links should disclose the next task and expected value: definition and sources, operating guidance, participation, or product boundary. | Engagement should not be optimized as time-on-page for its own sake or as an imitation of an infinite social feed. |
| **Credibility in use** | Stanford’s research summary recommends verifiable accuracy, named expertise and organization, clear contact paths, purpose-appropriate professional design, utility, recent review signals, promotional restraint, and error avoidance. [4] | Evidence, owner, date, update record, direct source links, useful task paths, and correction routes are higher-value trust assets than decorative status cues. | Authority should never be inferred from visual form, borrowed logos, fabricated testimonials, or unverified credential language. |
| **Color** | Labrecque and Milne’s article examines color in marketing; it is a specific empirical source, not support for a universal color-to-sales formula. [5] WCAG evaluates readability through luminance contrast rather than hue preference. [6] | Treat color as a consistent semantic system: orientation, state, relationship, action, and caution. Validate foreground/background contrast for its actual context. | No hue “induces trust,” “creates sales,” or works consistently across culture, category, device, or task. |
| **Typography** | A 246-participant e-commerce experiment comparing serif and sans-serif versions in one family did not find a general serif-versus-sans-serif usability advantage. [7] USWDS emphasizes size, measure, line-height, alignment, and spacing as readability controls. [8] | Choose type for precise role, readable measure, durable contrast, and screen behavior; use display typography sparingly and working text plainly. | “Serif equals authority” or “sans equals conversion” is not a valid implementation rule. |
| **Ethical engagement** | OECD and FTC guidance describe countdown pressure, hidden information, repeated nagging, subscription traps, forced registration, and privacy intrusions as manipulative digital patterns. [9] [10] | Attention should be earned with fresh evidence, a clear next question, saved progress, and explicit choice—not coerced via urgency theater, forced data collection, or cancellation friction. | ARM must not use behavioral targeting or engagement loops that obscure choice, create false scarcity, or make exit harder than entry. |

## Working design rules

> **Engagement quality is qualified return value:** a reader should be able to understand a claim faster, inspect why it is made, continue through an appropriate next task, and leave without friction.

1. The page opening should state the operating question, show one original system visual, and make one next task explicit.
2. The visual field should distinguish **orientation**, **evidence**, **decision**, **action**, and **review** without assigning credibility by color alone.
3. Cross-property links must name their reader benefit and relationship. They may offer a definition, executive operating guide, contribution pathway, or product boundary, but must not imply a synthetic endorsement or forced consolidation.
4. Attention should deepen through progressive disclosure: short premise, inspectable artifact, bounded exercise, related route. Avoid infinite scroll, unexplained recommendations, autoplay, or social-pressure counters.
5. Every visual asset must identify whether it is conceptual, documentary, sourced, or generated. Meaningful imagery must have accurate alternative text; decorative geometry must be silent to assistive technology.
